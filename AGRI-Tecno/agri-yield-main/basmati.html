🌾 Basmati Rice Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

▼
  • Basmati requires well-prepared, level, and fertile fields for uniform growth.
  • First ploughing should be deep with a mouldboard plough, followed by 2–3 harrowings.
  • Land should be well-puddled (wet tillage) before transplanting to reduce water loss and suppress weeds.
  • Ensure good drainage and prepare proper bunds (embankments) to hold irrigation water.

2. Soil Type and pH

▼
  • Best suited soils: Clay loam to silty loam with good water retention capacity.
  • Ideal pH: 5.5 – 7.5 (slightly acidic to neutral).
  • Avoid saline or alkaline soils as they reduce grain aroma and yield.

3. Climate Requirements

▼
  • Basmati is a tropical and subtropical crop, requires warm climate.
  • Temperature: 20°C to 35°C during growth; optimum is 25–32°C.
  • Rainfall: 100–150 cm is ideal (irrigation is needed in low rainfall areas).
  • Requires bright sunshine and dry weather during maturity for better aroma.
  • Cannot tolerate frost or cold stress.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

▼
  • Popular Basmati varieties: Pusa Basmati 1121, Pusa Basmati 1509, Pusa Basmati 1, Traditional Basmati.
  • Use certified seeds for high yield and quality.
  • Seed rate: 8–10 kg/acre for nursery sowing.
  • Seed treatment: Soak seeds in water for 8–10 hours and treat with fungicides (e.g., Carbendazim 2 g/kg seed) to prevent seed-borne diseases.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

▼
  • Nursery raising & transplanting is the most common method.
  • Prepare raised nursery beds of 1–1.5 m width.
  • Sow pre-germinated seeds evenly and cover with fine soil.
  • After 25–30 days, seedlings are ready for transplanting.
  • Transplant spacing: 20 x 15 cm or 22.5 x 22.5 cm (row-to-row × plant-to-plant).
  • Plant 2–3 seedlings per hill for optimum growth.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

▼
  • Organic manures: Apply 5–10 tons/acre of well-decomposed FYM (farmyard manure) before last ploughing.
  • Chemical fertilizers (per acre): Nitrogen (N): 40–50 kg, Phosphorus (P2O5): 20–25 kg, Potash (K2O): 20–25 kg
  • Apply half nitrogen + full phosphorus and potash as basal dose at transplanting.
  • Apply remaining nitrogen in 2 splits: First top dressing 25–30 days after transplanting, Second top dressing 50–55 days after transplanting.
  • Foliar spray of micronutrients (zinc, iron, boron) helps improve yield and grain quality.

7. Irrigation Management

▼
  • First irrigation: Immediately after transplanting.
  • Maintain 2–5 cm water depth until flowering.
  • During flowering and grain filling, maintain soil moisture without water stress.
  • Drain water before 15–20 days of harvesting to ease harvesting and avoid grain shattering.

8. Weed and Pest Control

▼
  • Major weeds: Echinochloa, Cyperus, Fimbristylis.
  • Apply pre-emergence herbicide (Butachlor 1.5 kg a.i./ha) within 2–3 days of transplanting.
  • Hand weeding at 20 and 40 days after transplanting.
  • Pests: Stem borer, leaf folder, brown planthopper (BPH), rice hispa. Use pheromone traps and spray insecticides when infestation crosses ETL.
  • Diseases: Blast, bacterial leaf blight, sheath blight. Use resistant varieties and treat with fungicides.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

▼
  • Harvest when 80–85% of the grains are mature and straw turns yellow.
  • Avoid early harvesting, as immature grains reduce aroma and cooking quality.
  • Harvesting is done by sickle or combine harvester.
  • Average yield: 18–25 quintals/acre.
  • Traditional Basmati yields less but fetches higher price due to aroma.

10. Post-Harvest Handling

▼
  • Sun-dry bundles for 2–3 days to reduce grain moisture to 13–14%.
  • Threshing manually or by machine.
  • Store grains in aerated godowns with moisture-proof bags.
  • Aging: 6–12 months before milling enhances aroma and elongation.
  • Proper milling with minimal breakage is essential for premium quality.
