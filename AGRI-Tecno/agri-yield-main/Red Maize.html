🌽 Red Maize Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

Land should be deep ploughed once with a mouldboard plough, followed by 2–3 harrowings to get a fine tilth.

Remove weeds and crop residues before sowing.

Apply 10–12 tonnes/ha of well-decomposed FYM or compost during the last ploughing.

In areas with high rainfall, prepare raised beds or ridges to avoid water stagnation.

2. Soil Type and pH

Performs best in fertile loamy or sandy loam soils with good organic matter.

Not suitable for saline or highly acidic soils.

Ideal soil pH: 6.0–7.5.

3. Climate Requirements

Red maize is hardy and grown under both rainfed and irrigated conditions.

Best suited to warm tropical and subtropical climates.

Optimum temperature: 18–28°C.

Rainfall: 50–80 cm, evenly distributed.

Sensitive to frost but more tolerant to drought compared to hybrids.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

Red maize is usually a traditional/local variety with colored kernels.

Seed rate: 20–25 kg/ha.

Seed treatment:

  • Carbendazim or Thiram @ 2–3 g/kg seed against fungal diseases.
  • Inoculate with Azospirillum or Azotobacter for nitrogen fixation.
  • In pest-prone soils, use Imidacloprid 5–10 ml/kg seed.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

Direct sowing by dibbling or seed drill.

Spacing: 60 × 25 cm (can be wider in rainfed areas).

Planting depth: 3–4 cm in heavy soil, 4–5 cm in sandy soil.

Ensure uniform stand for proper cob development.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

Red maize has moderate nutrient requirement compared to hybrids.

Basal dose: FYM 10–12 t/ha + NPK (40:40:20 kg/ha).

Top dressing: Additional 40–50 kg N/ha in 2 splits:

  • Knee-high stage (25–30 DAS)
  • Tasseling stage

Micronutrient sprays (ZnSO₄ 25 kg/ha) improve kernel color and quality.

7. Irrigation Management

Under rainfed conditions, it survives with minimal irrigation.

In irrigated lands: provide 4–5 irrigations at critical stages:

  • Germination
  • Knee-high stage
  • Tasseling
  • Silking
  • Grain filling

Avoid waterlogging in early stages.

8. Weed and Pest Control

Weeds: First weeding at 20 DAS, second at 40 DAS. Pre-emergence Atrazine @ 1 kg a.i./ha helps control weeds.

Pests: Stem borer & fall armyworm are common. Spray Emamectin benzoate or Spinosad at early infestation.

Diseases: Relatively hardy but can be affected by leaf blight or rust. Spray Mancozeb or Propiconazole when symptoms appear.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

Crop duration: 95–110 days depending on season.

Harvest when husk turns brown and kernels harden.

Yield: Traditional red maize: 20–30 q/ha grain. Fodder yield: 30–40 t/ha green fodder.

Grain has deep red kernels rich in anthocyanins and antioxidants.

10. Post-Harvest Handling

Shelling: By hand or mechanical sheller.

Drying: Dry to 12–13% moisture for safe storage.

Storage: Store in clean bins; protects well due to harder kernels.

Uses: Ground into flour, porridge, traditional food, poultry feed, nutraceuticals (antioxidant-rich).
