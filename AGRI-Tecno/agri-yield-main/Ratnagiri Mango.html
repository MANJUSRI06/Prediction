🌱 Ratnagiri Alphonso Mango Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

  • Deep ploughing (1–2 times) to loosen soil.
  • Remove weeds, stones, and stumps.
  • Prepare pits of 1 × 1 × 1 m at 10 × 10 m spacing.
  • Fill pits with topsoil + FYM (25–30 kg) + neem cake (1–2 kg) + single super phosphate (1 kg).
  • Ratnagiri’s lateritic soil and coastal climate provide natural advantage.

2. Soil Type and pH

  • Best suited for well-drained lateritic, sandy loam, or black soils.
  • Ideal pH: 5.5 – 7.5.
  • Avoid heavy clay or waterlogged soils.

3. Climate Requirements

  • Thrives in tropical coastal climate of Konkan region.
  • Optimum temperature: 24–32°C.
  • Annual rainfall: 2000–2500 mm (monsoon-fed).
  • Requires dry spell before flowering for good fruit set.
  • Sensitive to frost.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

  • Variety: Ratnagiri Alphonso (GI-tagged Hapus mango).
  • Propagation by softwood or stone grafting.
  • Grafts from certified nurseries preferred.
  • Treat grafts with Carbendazim (1 g/L) or Bordeaux mixture before planting to prevent fungal infection.

5. Planting Method and Spacing

  • Planting season: June–August (monsoon planting).
  • Spacing: 10 × 10 m (100 trees/ha).
  • Square system or contour planting in slopes.
  • Support grafts with stakes in early years.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

  • FYM: 25–30 kg/tree/year.
  • NPK schedule (per tree):
  • 1st year: 100 g N, 50 g P, 100 g K.
  • Increase gradually with age.
  • Mature trees: 1 kg N, 500 g P, 1 kg K annually.
  • Apply fertilizers in two splits: pre-flowering and after fruit set.
  • Micronutrients: Foliar spray of ZnSO₄ (0.5%) + Borax (0.2%) improves fruit size and quality.

7. Irrigation Management

  • Young plants: irrigate every 7–10 days in dry season.
  • Bearing trees: irrigate at critical stages – flowering, fruit set, fruit growth.
  • Stop irrigation 15–20 days before flowering to induce profuse bloom.
  • Drip irrigation recommended for water efficiency.

8. Weed and Pest Control

  • Mulching with dry leaves or grass helps moisture conservation.
  • Common pests: Mango hopper, fruit fly, mealy bug – controlled with neem oil (3–5%) or recommended insecticides.
  • Diseases: Powdery mildew, anthracnose, die-back – managed with Mancozeb or Carbendazim sprays.

9. Harvesting and Yield

  • Bearing age: 5–6 years after planting.
  • Fruit maturity: 100–120 days after fruit set.
  • Average yield: 8–10 t/ha in early years; up to 12–15 t/ha at full bearing.
  • Fruits are medium-sized (150–300 g), golden-yellow with saffron pulp, rich aroma, and fibreless texture.

10. Post-harvest Handling

  • Harvest fruits at mature green stage when shoulders rise and base turns yellowish.
  • Handle carefully to avoid sap burn.
  • Pre-cooling at 12–15°C increases shelf life.
  • Pack in ventilated wooden/plastic crates with cushioning.
  • Storage: 12–15°C and 85–90% RH → shelf life 2–3 weeks.
  • Preferred for table use and premium export markets.
