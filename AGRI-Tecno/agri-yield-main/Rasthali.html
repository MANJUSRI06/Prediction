🍌 Rasthali Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

Land should be well-drained and fertile.

Deep ploughing (2–3 times) is required to remove weeds and loosen the soil.

Prepare pits of size 60 × 60 × 60 cm, keep them open for 2–3 weeks to expose pests and pathogens.

Mix pit soil with FYM (Farm Yard Manure), neem cake, and sand for better aeration and fertility.

2. Soil Type and pH

Best suited soil: Loamy, alluvial, or red laterite soils with good drainage.

Ideal pH: 6.0 – 7.5.

Avoid saline or waterlogged soils.

3. Climate Requirements

Grows well in tropical and subtropical regions.

Optimum temperature: 20°C – 30°C.

Requires an annual rainfall of 1000–2500 mm or assured irrigation.

Sensitive to drought and strong winds (provide windbreaks).

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

Propagation: Through healthy suckers or tissue culture plantlets.

Select sword suckers (1.5–2 kg) from disease-free plantations.

Treat suckers/plantlets with Carbendazim (0.1%) or Trichoderma solution before planting to prevent fungal infections.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

Planting season: April–June (rainfed) and October–November (irrigated).

Spacing:

  • 2.0 × 2.0 m under normal planting.
  • 1.8 × 1.8 m for high-density planting.

Depth: Plant suckers/tissue culture plants at 15–20 cm depth in prepared pits.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

Basal application: 10–15 kg FYM + 200 g Neem cake per pit.

Recommended dose (per plant):

  • 200–250 g N
  • 60–80 g P
  • 200–250 g K

Split into 3–4 applications (30, 60, 90 days after planting).

Apply micronutrients (Zn, B, Mg, Ca) for better fruit quality.

7. Irrigation Management

Requires weekly irrigation in summer and 10–15 days interval in winter.

Drip irrigation is highly recommended for water saving and better yield.

Avoid waterlogging, as Rasthali is sensitive to root rot.

8. Weed and Pest Control

Mulching with dry leaves or coir pith helps suppress weeds.

Intercropping with short-duration legumes also reduces weed growth.

Common pests: Banana stem borer, aphids, nematodes.
Control: Neem oil spray / Pseudomonas bio-control.

Common diseases: Panama wilt, Sigatoka leaf spot, Banana bunchy top virus (BBTV).
Control: Disease-free planting materials, timely fungicide sprays, and rogueing of affected plants.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

Crop duration: 12–14 months after planting.

Harvest when the bunch turns light green with 75–80% maturity.

Yield: 30–40 tonnes/ha under good management.

10. Post-harvest Handling

Harvest with 30–40 cm stalk attached.

Fruits should be ripened naturally in ventilated rooms or using ethylene-based ripening chambers (avoid calcium carbide).

Graded fruits should be packed in padded cartons or baskets to avoid bruising.

Storage: 13–15°C with 85–90% RH extends shelf life to 2–3 weeks.
