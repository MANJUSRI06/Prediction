🍈 Hybrid Jackfruit Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

Select well-drained fertile land with good sunlight.

Dig pits of 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft, fill with topsoil + FYM (10–15 kg) + Neem cake (1–2 kg).

Allow pits to weather for 2–3 weeks before planting.

2. Soil Type and pH

Best in loamy, sandy loam, or lateritic soils.

Soil pH: 6.0 – 7.5 (slightly acidic to neutral).

3. Climate Requirements

Grows well in tropical and subtropical regions.

Ideal temperature: 20°C – 35°C.

Requires 1200–2000 mm annual rainfall; sensitive to prolonged waterlogging.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

Hybrid varieties are propagated mainly by grafting or budding to maintain uniformity.

Rootstock: Seedlings from healthy jackfruit trees.

Scions: Elite hybrid variety with early-bearing and high-yield traits.

Treat grafts/seeds with Trichoderma / fungicide before planting.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

Plant grafts in prepared pits at 7 m × 7 m spacing (200 trees/ha).

Spacing may be adjusted to 8 m × 8 m for vigorous types.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

Apply FYM (20–25 kg/tree/year) + Neem cake (2 kg).

Recommended NPK (per tree/year):

  • Nitrogen: 200–300 g
  • Phosphorus: 150–200 g
  • Potassium: 200–300 g

Apply in two split doses (June–July and December–January).

7. Irrigation Management

Young plants need irrigation once in 7–10 days during summer.

Mature trees need watering at fruit development stage for better yield.

Avoid waterlogging; adopt mulching with dry leaves/coconut husks to conserve moisture.

8. Weed and Pest Control

Regular weeding around basin, especially in first 2–3 years.

Pests: fruit borer, mealy bugs – controlled by neem oil spray (3%) or biological control.

Diseases: fruit rot, stem rot – prevented by applying 1% Bordeaux mixture and good drainage.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

Hybrid grafted trees start bearing from 3rd–4th year (earlier than traditional types).

Peak fruiting: March–June and sometimes an additional flush depending on region.

Yield: 70–100 fruits per tree annually (8–20 kg per fruit).

Estimated yield: 25–30 tonnes/ha under good management.

10. Post-harvest Handling

Harvest fruits when they emit aroma and produce dull hollow sound on tapping.

Handle carefully to avoid cracks.

Fruits can be stored 5–7 days at room temperature, 2–3 weeks under cold storage.

Suitable for fresh consumption, chips, pulp, candies, and export packaging.
