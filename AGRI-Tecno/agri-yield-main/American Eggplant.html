🌱 American Eggplant Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

▼

Select well-drained fertile land with good organic content. Eggplant prefers loamy soils enriched with compost.

Deep plough the land 2–3 times to break clods and ensure fine tilth. Remove weeds and previous crop residues.

Apply 20–25 tons of well-decomposed farmyard manure (FYM) or compost per hectare during land preparation.

Raised beds or ridges and furrows are recommended to avoid water stagnation, which can damage roots.

2. Soil Type and pH

▼

Best suited for sandy loam to clay loam soils with high fertility.

Soil pH requirement: 5.5 – 6.8 (slightly acidic to neutral).

Avoid saline or highly alkaline soils. If soil is acidic, apply lime before sowing.

3. Climate Requirements

▼

Grows best in warm and frost-free climates.

Optimal temperature: 22–30°C for germination and vegetative growth.

Sensitive to extreme cold (below 15°C) and extreme heat (above 35°C).

Requires 6–8 hours of direct sunlight daily for proper fruit set.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

▼

Popular American varieties: Black Beauty, Florida High Bush, Early Long Purple, Black Bell, Dusky, Classic Hybrid.

Seed rate: 300–400 g per acre (for nursery).

Treat seeds with Trichoderma viride (4 g/kg) or carbendazim (2 g/kg) to prevent seed-borne diseases.

Priming seeds in warm water (50°C for 30 minutes) improves germination.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

▼

Seeds are first raised in a nursery bed. Transplanting is done at 4–5 weeks when seedlings are 12–15 cm tall.

Prepare nursery beds of 1 m width and convenient length, enriched with FYM and sand.

Spacing for transplanting:

  • Row-to-row: 60–75 cm
  • Plant-to-plant: 45–60 cm

Recommended plant population: 8,000–10,000 plants per acre.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

▼

Apply FYM/compost 20–25 tons per hectare as basal.

Fertilizer dose per hectare: NPK: 120:80:60 kg

Application:

  • Apply full P and K + ¼ N as basal dose.
  • Remaining N in 3 equal splits: 30, 60, and 90 days after transplanting.

Foliar spray of micronutrients like boron, zinc, and magnesium improves fruit set and quality.

7. Irrigation Management

▼

Requires moderate but regular irrigation.

Irrigate immediately after transplanting, then once every 7–10 days (more frequently in sandy soils or summer).

Avoid over-irrigation as it encourages wilt and root rot.

Drip irrigation with mulching conserves water and reduces weeds.

8. Weed and Pest Control

▼

Manual weeding at 20 and 40 days after transplanting is essential. Mulching (organic or plastic) reduces weed growth.

Major pests:

  • Shoot and fruit borer – use pheromone traps, neem oil sprays, or biocontrol (Trichogramma).
  • Aphids and whiteflies – control with neem oil, imidacloprid, or yellow sticky traps.

Major diseases:

  • Bacterial wilt – avoid waterlogging, use resistant varieties.
  • Phomopsis blight and leaf spot – spray carbendazim or mancozeb.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

▼

First harvest: 60–70 days after transplanting depending on variety.

Fruits should be harvested when they are shiny, firm, and immature (before seeds harden).

Harvest at 7–10 day intervals to promote continuous flowering and fruiting.

Yield potential:

  • 20–25 tons per hectare under good management.
  • Hybrids may yield up to 35–40 tons per hectare.

10. Post-Harvest Handling

▼

Handle fruits carefully to avoid bruising.

Sort and grade fruits based on size, color, and quality.

Store at 10–12°C with 90–95% relative humidity to extend shelf life (up to 7–10 days).

Can be transported in ventilated crates or bamboo baskets lined with paper.
