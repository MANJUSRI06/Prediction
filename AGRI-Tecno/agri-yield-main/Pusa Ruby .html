🍅 Pusa Ruby Tomato Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

Soil must be well-drained, fertile sandy loam / loamy soil.

Plough land 3–4 times, remove weeds & residues.

Beds/ridges & furrows system recommended.

Apply FYM/compost @ 20–25 t/ha at the time of final ploughing.

Soil sterilization (solarization or neem cake application) prevents nematodes & wilt disease.

2. Soil Type and pH

Suitable soils: Sandy loam, red loam, or black soils with good organic matter.

pH requirement: 6.0 – 7.5.

Heavy clay soils → poor root growth & waterlogging (not suitable).

3. Climate Requirements

Tomato is a warm-season crop but sensitive to extremes.

Optimum temperature:

  • Germination: 20–25°C
  • Growth & flowering: 20–30°C
  • Fruit set: 15–25°C

Temperature >35°C or <10°C → poor fruit set.

Requires plenty of sunshine; high humidity favours fungal diseases.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

Variety: Pusa Ruby (Developed by IARI, Delhi).

Characteristics:

  • Early maturing variety.
  • Plants are medium tall, determinate growth habit.
  • Fruits are small, round, and attractive red.
  • Good for table use & salad.

Seed rate: 250–300 g/ha (nursery raised).

Seed treatment:

  • Thiram/Carbendazim @ 2–3 g/kg seed.
  • Trichoderma treatment eco-friendly option.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

Nursery raising: Seeds sown in raised beds, covered lightly with soil + straw mulch.

Seedlings ready in 25–30 days (4–5 leaf stage).

Transplanting: At 25–30 days, healthy seedlings transplanted.

Spacing:

  • 60 × 45 cm (row × plant) for normal planting.
  • 75 × 60 cm for staked/trellised crop.

Depth: Shallow planting → collar region just above soil.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

FYM/compost: 20–25 t/ha.

NPK recommendation (per hectare):

  • Nitrogen → 100–120 kg
  • Phosphorus (P₂O₅) → 60 kg
  • Potassium (K₂O) → 60 kg

Application method:

  • Basal: Full P + K + ½ N before transplanting.
  • Top dressing: Remaining N in 2–3 splits at flowering & fruiting stages.

Foliar spray: 1% urea or micronutrients (Zn, B, Ca) improves fruit set & quality.

7. Irrigation Management

First irrigation: immediately after transplanting.

Frequency: every 7–10 days in winter, 5–6 days in summer.

Critical stages: flowering, fruit set, fruit development.

Avoid over-irrigation → cracking of fruits.

Drip irrigation recommended → water saving + higher yield.

8. Weed and Pest Control

Weeds:

  • 2–3 hand weedings (20, 40, 60 DAT).
  • Mulching (plastic/straw) controls weeds & conserves moisture.
  • Pre-emergence herbicide: Pendimethalin.

Pests:

  • Fruit borer (Helicoverpa) → pheromone traps, neem oil sprays, Emamectin benzoate.
  • Whitefly → vector of leaf curl virus. Yellow sticky traps + Imidacloprid.
  • Aphids & thrips → controlled by neem-based sprays.

Diseases:

  • Early blight → brown concentric rings. Spray Mancozeb/Chlorothalonil.
  • Late blight → water-soaked lesions. Spray Metalaxyl + Mancozeb.
  • Leaf curl virus → remove infected plants, control whiteflies, use resistant varieties.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

Maturity: 100–120 days after sowing (70–80 days after transplanting).

Fruits harvested at different stages:

  • Green mature for distant markets.
  • Breaker stage (red tinge at blossom end) for local markets.

Yield:

  • Average: 25–30 tonnes/ha.
  • Good management: up to 35–40 t/ha.

10. Post-Harvest Handling

Grading: Based on size, shape, and colour.

Packing: Bamboo baskets/cartons lined with paper.

Storage:

  • At room temperature: 4–5 days.
  • In cold storage (12–15°C, 85–90% RH): up to 2–3 weeks.

For long-distance transport → harvest at breaker stage.

✅ Quick Notes (Revision)

  • Soil: Sandy loam, pH 6–7.5.
  • Climate: 20–30°C, sensitive to extremes.
  • Variety: Pusa Ruby (early, red, round fruits).
  • Seed rate: 250–300 g/ha.
  • Spacing: 60×45 cm.
  • Fertilizer: 100:60:60 NPK + FYM 25 t/ha.
  • Irrigation: 7–10 days interval; critical at flowering & fruiting.
  • Pests: fruit borer, whitefly.
  • Diseases: early blight, leaf curl virus.
  • Yield: 25–30 t/ha.
  • Storage: 4–5 days at room temp, 2–3 weeks in cold storage.
