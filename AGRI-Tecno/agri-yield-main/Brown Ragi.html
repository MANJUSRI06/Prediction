🌱 Brown Ragi (Finger Millet – Brown Variety) Cultivation Guide
Jump to:

1. Land and Soil Preparation

▼

Prepare the field with 2–3 deep ploughings followed by harrowing to ensure fine tilth.

Incorporate well-decomposed FYM @ 8–10 tons/acre at the last ploughing.

Level the field properly to avoid water stagnation.

Brown ragi is hardy and can grow in marginal and less fertile soils, ideal for upland and semi-arid areas.

2. Soil Type and pH

▼

Thrives in red loamy, light black, and sandy loam soils.

Well-drained soils are preferred; waterlogging reduces yield and increases disease incidence.

Soil pH: 5.5–7.5 (slightly acidic to neutral).

Can tolerate low fertility soils but performs best with nutrient-rich soils.

3. Climate Requirements

▼

Brown ragi is drought-tolerant and suitable for semi-arid and hilly tracts.

Temperature: 25–30°C is optimum.

Rainfall requirement: 400–900 mm annually; can survive dry periods.

Grows well at altitudes up to 1800–2000 m above sea level.

Sensitive to frost at flowering and grain-filling stages.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

▼

Popular local varieties: Karunkezhvaragu (Tamil Nadu), Kappu Ragi (Karnataka).

Seed rate: 8–10 kg/acre for line sowing, 10–12 kg/acre for broadcasting.

Seed treatment:

  • Treat with Trichoderma viride @ 4 g/kg or Carbendazim 2 g/kg.
  • Biofertilizer coating with Azospirillum + Phosphobacteria improves germination and early growth.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

▼

Season:

  • Kharif: June–July (rainy season)
  • Rabi: September–October under irrigated conditions

Methods:

  • Line sowing – Recommended for uniform plant population and easy weed management.
  • Broadcasting – Traditional, less efficient.

Spacing: 30 cm between rows × 10–12 cm between plants. Thin seedlings at 2 weeks.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

▼

Apply FYM @ 8–10 tons/acre before sowing.

NPK recommendation: 40:20:20 kg/acre.

Apply half nitrogen + full P and K as basal at sowing.

Remaining nitrogen top-dressed at 30–35 DAS (tillering stage).

Foliar spray of 2% urea at flowering enhances grain filling. Micronutrients like zinc and boron may be applied in deficient soils.

7. Irrigation Management

▼

Brown ragi is drought-tolerant, but irrigation improves yield:

  • At sowing (if rainfall insufficient)
  • At tillering (25–30 DAS)
  • At flowering (45–50 DAS)
  • Grain-filling stage (65–70 DAS)

Avoid waterlogging at all stages.

8. Weed and Pest Control

▼

First weeding: 15–20 DAS. Second weeding: 30–35 DAS.

Herbicide option: Pendimethalin 1.0 kg a.i./ha (pre-emergence).

Pests: Shoot fly – early sowing and Neem oil sprays help. Stem borer – Neem-based or chemical sprays if severe.

Diseases: Leaf and neck blast – Tricyclazole 0.6 g/litre or Carbendazim 1 g/litre. Smut – seed treatment with Carbendazim.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

▼

Crop duration: 100–120 days depending on variety and season.

Harvest when ear heads turn dark brown and grains harden.

Cut ear heads separately or harvest whole plants and sun-dry before threshing.

Yield: Grain 8–12 quintals/acre (800–1200 kg), Straw 15–20 quintals/acre (fodder).

10. Post-Harvest Handling

▼

Dry ear heads for 3–4 days in sunlight before threshing.

Thresh manually or using mechanical thresher.

Dry grains to 8–10% moisture for safe storage.

Store in airtight bins or gunny bags in a dry, ventilated place.

Brown ragi is used for traditional foods, roti, porridge, flour, malt, and health mixes, often preferred for its rich mineral content and slightly stronger taste.
