🧅 Red Onion Cultivation in India – Detailed Guide

🌱 Land and Soil Preparation

  • Soil must be loose, well-drained, and free from stones.
  • 3–4 ploughings with harrowing for fine tilth.
  • Raised beds / ridges & furrows method for drainage.
  • Apply FYM/compost 20–25 t/ha before last plough.
  • Ensure no standing water → onions are very sensitive to waterlogging.
  • Soil solarization (using polythene sheets for 2–3 weeks) reduces soil-borne pests & weeds.

🧪 Soil Type and pH

  • Best suited soil: sandy loam, silt loam, clay loam.
  • pH range: 6.0 – 7.5.
  • Avoid heavy clay soils → hinder bulb expansion.
  • Avoid saline/alkaline soils → poor bulb quality.
  • Organic matter-rich soils give better yield & bulb size.

🌤 Climate Requirements

  • Onion is a cool-season crop during vegetative growth.
  • Needs dry weather at maturity for bulb storage quality.
  • Germination temperature: 15–20°C.
  • Growth stage: 13–24°C.
  • Bulb initiation & enlargement: 20–25°C.
  • Excessive rain/humidity → fungal diseases (purple blotch, downy mildew).
  • Hot (>35°C) + dry winds → small bulbs & bolting.

🌰 Seed Variety and Treatment

  • Varieties in India: Early season (Pusa Red, N-53, Agrifound Light Red), Late season (Arka Kalyan, Arka Niketan, Bhima Red, Pusa Riddhi).
  • Seed rate: 8–10 kg/ha for transplanting; 12–15 kg/ha for direct sowing.
  • Seed treatment: Thiram (2g/kg) or Carbendazim (1g/kg) to prevent damping-off.
  • Trichoderma viride treatment (5g/kg) → eco-friendly biocontrol.
  • Seed priming with water (6 hrs) → faster germination.

📏 Sowing Method and Spacing

  • Nursery beds: 1m width, 3–4m length; mix FYM + neem cake; cover seeds with fine soil + straw mulch.
  • Transplant seedlings at 6–7 weeks (pencil-thickness size).
  • Spacing: Row-to-row 15 cm, Plant-to-plant 10 cm, Depth 2–3 cm max.

💊 Fertilizer Schedule

  • FYM/Compost: 20–25 t/ha.
  • NPK (kg/ha): 100–120 : 50–60 : 50–60.
  • Application: Full P & K + half N → basal; Remaining N → split at 30 and 45 days.
  • Micronutrients: ZnSO₄ (25 kg/ha), Borax (10 kg/ha) → better bulb quality.
  • Foliar spray of micronutrients improves yield. Excess N → thick neck & poor storage quality.

💧 Irrigation Management

  • Light, frequent irrigations preferred.
  • First irrigation immediately after transplanting.
  • Subsequent irrigation: Winter 8–10 days, Summer 6–7 days.
  • Critical stages: bulb initiation + development.
  • Stop irrigation 10–12 days before harvesting → bulbs dry properly.
  • Drip irrigation → saves 40% water, improves bulb size.

🐛 Weed and Pest Control

  • Weeds: Hand weeding at 20 & 40 days; mulching reduces weed growth.
  • Pre-emergence herbicide: Pendimethalin (1 kg a.i./ha).
  • Pests: Thrips, Onion maggot, Aphids – use neem oil, Imidacloprid, Chlorpyriphos as recommended.
  • Diseases: Purple blotch, Downy mildew, Stemphylium blight – rotate fungicides.

⏳ Harvesting Time and Yield

  • Duration: 100–120 days after transplanting.
  • Harvest indicators: 70–80% tops yellow & fall, neck shrivels.
  • Harvest by gently pulling bulbs.
  • Yield: Average 25–30 t/ha, High-input 35–40 t/ha.

📦 Post-Harvest Handling

  • Curing: Sun dry bulbs 7–10 days → outer scales dry, neck closes.
  • Cleaning: Remove roots & tops (leave 2–3 cm neck).
  • Grading: Sort based on size, remove damaged bulbs.
  • Storage: Well-ventilated dry godowns, bamboo/mud structures, or modern ventilated onion storage houses.
  • Shelf life: 3–4 months in proper storage.
