🍅 Rupali Tomato Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

Well-drained sandy loam soil with good organic matter.

Plough the land 2–3 times to make it fine tilth.

Apply well-decomposed FYM (20–25 tons/ha) during final ploughing.

Raised beds or ridges help in good aeration and avoid waterlogging.

2. Soil Type and pH

Best in sandy loam to clay loam soils.

Soil pH should be 6.0–7.5 (slightly acidic to neutral).

Avoid saline/alkaline soils.

3. Climate Requirements

Grows well in warm, frost-free climate.

Optimum temperature: 20–25°C for growth, 15–20°C for fruit setting.

Sensitive to very high temperature (>35°C) and frost.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

Rupali Tomato is a hybrid, high-yielding variety.

Fruits: oval-shaped, attractive red color, uniform size, average weight 80–100 g.

Yield potential: 65–75 tons/ha under good management.

Seed treatment:

  • Trichoderma viride (4 g/kg seed) or Carbendazim (2 g/kg seed)

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

Nursery sowing is preferred → seedlings transplanted after 25–30 days.

Seed rate: 200–250 g/ha.

Spacing:

  • Row to row: 90–120 cm
  • Plant to plant: 45–60 cm

Planting on ridges helps in proper drainage.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

Basal dose: 20 tons FYM + 40:60:60 kg NPK/ha.

Top dressing:

  • 25% N at 30 days after transplanting
  • 25% N + 50% K at 60 days
  • Remaining 25% N + 50% K at 90 days

Micronutrient sprays (Zn, B, Ca, Mg) improve fruit setting & quality.

Calcium nitrate sprays reduce blossom end rot.

7. Irrigation Management

First irrigation immediately after transplanting.

Weekly irrigation in summer, every 10–12 days in winter.

Drip irrigation recommended for higher yield & water efficiency.

Avoid water stagnation → causes root rot & fruit cracking.

8. Weed and Pest Control

Weeds: 2–3 manual weedings or mulching with plastic film.

Pests:

  • Fruit borer – Spray Neem oil 5% or Emamectin benzoate
  • Whiteflies/aphids – Spray Imidacloprid 0.3 ml/L

Diseases:

  • Early blight – Mancozeb spray
  • Leaf curl (viral) – Control vector (whiteflies)
  • Powdery mildew – Sulphur dusting or Hexaconazole spray

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

First harvest after 60–65 days of transplanting.

Fruits harvested at breaker stage (slight red color) for long-distance transport.

Yield: 65–75 tons/ha under good management.

10. Post-Harvest Handling

Harvest fruits at mature green or breaker stage for storage/transport.

Grade fruits based on size and color.

Store at 12–15°C to extend shelf life.

Pack in ventilated crates/cartons for transport.
