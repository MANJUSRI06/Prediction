🌾 Seeragasamba Rice Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

  • Seeragasamba is a traditional aromatic rice native to Tamil Nadu, especially Salem and Namakkal.
  • Plough the field 2–3 times deeply to break clods and allow aeration.
  • Puddling is done by flooding and softening the soil.
  • Level the field for uniform water distribution.
  • Construct strong bunds to retain water and arrange channels for controlled irrigation.
  • Apply FYM, compost, or green manure 2–3 weeks before transplanting to enhance fertility and aroma.

2. Soil Type and pH

  • Preferred soil: Fertile clay loam or sandy loam with good water retention.
  • pH: 5.5 – 7.5 (slightly acidic to neutral).
  • Seeragasamba is sensitive to saline soils; low-salinity fields are preferred.

3. Climate Requirements

  • Warm and humid tropical climate required.
  • Ideal temperature: 20–35°C; cooler nights enhance aroma.
  • Annual rainfall: 100–120 cm; controlled irrigation is essential.
  • Traditionally grown in Samba season (Aug–Jan), duration 135–150 days.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

  • Use pure Seeragasamba seeds from certified sources.
  • Seed rate: 20–25 kg/ha.
  • Soak seeds in 10% salt solution, wash, and soak 12 hours.
  • Treat seeds with bio-fungicides (Trichoderma viride) or chemical fungicides (Carbendazim 2 g/kg).
  • Pre-germinate seeds before nursery sowing.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

  • Nursery: Raised mat or wet-bed nursery, 500–600 m² per hectare.
  • Transplant seedlings at 20–25 days old.
  • Spacing: Normal: 20×15 cm, SRI: 25×25 cm with single seedling/hill.
  • Transplant depth: 2–3 cm to ensure strong establishment.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

  • Highly responsive to organic nutrition, enhancing aroma and quality.
  • Basal: FYM/compost 10–12 tons/ha, neem cake 250 kg/ha.
  • Chemical fertilizers per hectare: N 120 kg, P₂O₅ 50 kg, K₂O 50 kg.
  • Split application: 50% N + full P + 50% K at transplanting, 25% N + 25% K at tillering, 25% N at panicle initiation.
  • Bio-fertilizers: Azospirillum and Phosphobacteria improve nutrient uptake.

7. Irrigation Management

  • Continuous but controlled irrigation required.
  • Maintain 2–5 cm standing water after transplanting.
  • Critical stages: tillering, panicle initiation, flowering.
  • SRI method: alternate wetting and drying improves roots and saves water.
  • Drain water at maturity to prevent lodging and grain discoloration.

8. Weed and Pest Control

  • Weeds: Pre-emergence herbicide Butachlor (1.5 L/ha) 3–5 days after transplanting.
  • Hand/mechanical weeding at 20 and 40 days.
  • Pests: Stem borer, leaf folder, brown plant hopper; controlled using recommended sprays and neem products.
  • Diseases: Blast (Tricyclazole), Sheath blight (Validamycin), Bacterial leaf blight (Pseudomonas fluorescens seed treatment).

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

  • Duration: 135–150 days after sowing.
  • Indicators: grains turn golden-yellow, straw dry, 80–85% grains mature.
  • Harvest with sickles or harvesters, thresh mechanically, sun-dry to 12–14% moisture.
  • Yield: 3.5–4.5 tons/ha normally; up to 5 tons/ha with SRI and improved management.

10. Post-Harvest Handling

  • Sun-dry grains 2–3 days to 12% moisture.
  • Store in ventilated, pest-free godowns with clean gunny bags; use neem leaves if needed.
  • Semi-polish traditionally to retain aroma and nutrition.
  • Used for biryani, pulao, and special Tamil Nadu dishes due to small grain size and fragrance.
