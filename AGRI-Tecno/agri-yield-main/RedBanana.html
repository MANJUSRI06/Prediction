🍌 Red Banana Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

Land should be deep, fertile, and well-drained.

Perform 2–3 deep ploughings to remove weeds and improve soil structure.

Prepare pits of 60 × 60 × 60 cm, expose them for 2–3 weeks to sunlight to kill pests and pathogens.

Mix pit soil with FYM (Farm Yard Manure), neem cake, and sand for better aeration, nutrition, and disease resistance.

2. Soil Type and pH

Best soils: Red loamy, alluvial, or laterite soils with good drainage.

Ideal pH: 6.0 – 7.5.

Avoid saline, alkaline, and waterlogged soils.

3. Climate Requirements

Thrives in tropical and subtropical climates.

Optimum temperature: 20°C – 30°C.

Rainfall requirement: 1000–2500 mm annually (or assured irrigation).

Sensitive to strong winds and prolonged drought → provide windbreaks and regular irrigation.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

Propagation: Healthy sword suckers (1.5–2.0 kg) or tissue culture plantlets.

Select disease-free planting materials from reliable sources.

Pre-treatment: Dip suckers in Carbendazim (0.1%) or Trichoderma solution to prevent fungal diseases.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

Planting seasons:

  • April–June (rainfed)
  • October–November (irrigated)

Spacing:

  • Normal: 2.0 × 2.0 m
  • High density: 1.8 × 1.8 m

Plant suckers/tissue culture plants at 15–20 cm depth in pits.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

Basal dose per pit: 10–15 kg FYM + 250 g Neem cake.

Per plant fertilizer requirement:

  • 200–250 g N
  • 60–80 g P
  • 200–250 g K

Apply fertilizers in 3–4 splits (30, 60, 90 days after planting).

Provide micronutrients (Zn, Mg, B, Ca) to improve fruit color, sweetness, and size.

7. Irrigation Management

Irrigate once every 7–8 days in summer and every 12–15 days in winter.

Drip irrigation is ideal for better water efficiency and yield.

Avoid water stagnation → red banana is prone to root and corm rot in waterlogged soils.

8. Weed and Pest Control

Mulching with dry leaves, straw, or coir pith conserves soil moisture and reduces weeds.

Intercropping with legumes improves soil fertility and suppresses weeds.

Common pests: Stem borer, aphids (vector of BBTV), nematodes.
Control: Neem oil spray, Pseudomonas, or Trichoderma application.

Diseases: Panama wilt, Sigatoka leaf spot, Bunchy Top Virus.
Control: Use resistant suckers, maintain field sanitation, remove diseased plants promptly.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

Crop duration: 14–16 months after planting (slightly longer than Rasthali).

Harvest when fruits are 75–80% mature and skin turns reddish-green.

Average yield: 30–35 tonnes/ha under good cultivation practices.

10. Post-harvest Handling

Harvest with a 30–40 cm stalk for easier handling.

Ripen fruits naturally in ventilated rooms or with ethylene ripening chambers (avoid calcium carbide).

Grading: Sort fruits based on size, color, and weight.

Packing: Use padded cartons or bamboo baskets to reduce bruising.

Storage: At 13–14°C with 85–90% RH, fruits stay fresh for 2–3 weeks.
