🌱 Red Ragi (Finger Millet – Red Variety) Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

Prepare the field with 2–3 deep ploughings followed by harrowing to get a fine tilth.

Incorporate FYM @ 8–12 tons/acre during the last ploughing to improve soil structure and moisture retention.

Level the land properly; form ridges and furrows or small beds depending on irrigation facilities.

Being a hardy crop, red ragi can be grown in marginal uplands where paddy cannot be cultivated.

2. Soil Type and pH

Prefers red loamy soils, sandy loams, and lateritic soils.

Can tolerate poor soils, but well-drained fertile soils give better yields.

Soil pH should be 5.5–7.5, slightly acidic to neutral.

Does not perform well in waterlogged conditions.

3. Climate Requirements

Red ragi is a tropical crop grown mainly in kharif season (rainy season), but also cultivated in rabi under irrigation.

Temperature: 25–30°C is optimum.

Rainfall requirement: 500–1000 mm annually, but it is highly drought-tolerant.

Best suited for semi-arid and hilly tracts up to 2000 m above sea level.

Sensitive to frost and prolonged cloudy weather at grain-filling stage.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

Popular traditional varieties: Indaf-8, Indaf-9, GPU-28, CO-15, TNAU Ragi-752, local landraces (Chem Ragi in Tamil Nadu, Kempu Ragi in Karnataka).

Seed rate: 8–10 kg/acre for line sowing, 10–12 kg/acre for broadcasting.

Seed treatment:

  • Treat seeds with Trichoderma viride @ 4 g/kg to prevent seed-borne diseases.
  • Coat seeds with Azospirillum and Phosphobacteria before sowing for better establishment.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

Season:

  • Kharif: June–July (with onset of monsoon)
  • Rabi: September–October (under irrigation)

Methods:

  • Broadcasting – Traditional, less efficient.
  • Line sowing – Preferred for better crop management.
  • Transplanting (nursery-raised seedlings) is also practiced for higher yields.

Spacing: 30 cm between rows × 10 cm between plants.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

Basal application of FYM/compost 8–10 tons/acre before sowing.

Recommended NPK dose: 40:20:20 kg/acre.

Apply half N + full P and K as basal.

Apply remaining N at 30–35 DAS (tillering stage).

Foliar spray of 1% urea or DAP solution at flowering stage helps grain development. Micronutrients like zinc and boron improve yield in deficient soils.

7. Irrigation Management

Red ragi is mostly grown as a rainfed crop, but under irrigation:

  • First irrigation: At sowing if rainfall is insufficient.
  • Second irrigation: Tillering stage (25–30 DAS)
  • Third irrigation: Flowering stage (45–50 DAS)
  • Fourth irrigation: Grain-filling stage (65–70 DAS)

Avoid waterlogging at all stages.

8. Weed and Pest Control

First weeding at 15–20 DAS. Second weeding at 30–35 DAS.

Herbicide option: Pendimethalin 1.0 kg a.i./ha as pre-emergence or manual weeding.

Pests: Shoot fly – dead hearts, managed by early sowing and Neem oil sprays. Stem borer – Carbofuran or Neem seed kernel extract.

Diseases: Leaf & neck blast – Tricyclazole 0.6 g/litre or Carbendazim 1 g/litre. Smut – treat seeds with Carbendazim.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

Crop duration: 100–120 days depending on variety and season.

Harvest when ears turn brown and grains harden.

Cut ear heads separately or harvest whole plants, then dry before threshing.

Yield potential: Grain 10–15 quintals/acre (1000–1500 kg), Straw 20–25 quintals/acre (fodder).

10. Post-Harvest Handling

Dry harvested ear heads for 3–5 days in sun before threshing.

Threshing done by beating with sticks or mechanical threshers.

Dry grains to 8–10% moisture before storage.

Store in airtight bins, mud pots, or gunny bags in a dry and well-ventilated godown.

Red ragi is mainly used for ragi mudde, roti, dosa, porridge, malt, and health mixes, in high demand in South India.
