🍌 Thenvalai Banana Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

Land should be fertile, well-drained, and rich in organic matter.

Carry out 2–3 deep ploughings to remove weeds and improve tilth.

Prepare pits of 60 × 60 × 60 cm and expose to sunlight for 2–3 weeks to kill soil-borne pests and pathogens.

Mix pit soil with FYM, neem cake, and sand for better aeration and nutrition.

2. Soil Type and pH

Best suited soils: Loamy, red, or alluvial soils with good drainage.

Ideal pH: 6.0 – 7.5.

Avoid saline or alkaline soils and areas with poor drainage.

3. Climate Requirements

Grows well in tropical and subtropical climates.

Optimum temperature: 20°C – 30°C.

Rainfall requirement: 1000–2000 mm annually (or assured irrigation).

Sensitive to drought and strong winds → provide windbreaks and regular irrigation.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

Propagation: By sword suckers (1.5–2.0 kg) or tissue culture plants.

Select healthy, disease-free planting material from reliable sources.

Treat suckers with Carbendazim (0.1%) or Trichoderma suspension before planting to avoid fungal diseases.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

Planting seasons:

  • April–June (rainfed)
  • October–November (irrigated)

Spacing:

  • Normal: 2.0 × 2.0 m
  • High density: 1.8 × 1.8 m

Plant suckers/tissue culture plants at a depth of 15–20 cm.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

Basal application per pit: 10–15 kg FYM + 200 g Neem cake.

Fertilizer dose per plant:

  • 200 g N
  • 60 g P
  • 200 g K

Apply in 3–4 splits (30, 60, 90 days after planting).

Supplement with micronutrients (Zn, B, Mg, Ca) for better sweetness and aroma.

7. Irrigation Management

Irrigate once in 7–8 days during summer and every 12–15 days in winter.

Drip irrigation is ideal for uniform growth and water saving.

Avoid water stagnation → leads to root and corm rot.

8. Weed and Pest Control

Mulching with dry leaves, paddy straw, or coir pith conserves moisture and reduces weeds.

Intercropping with legumes enriches soil and suppresses weeds.

Common pests: Banana stem borer, aphids, nematodes.
Control: Neem oil spray, biological controls like Pseudomonas and Trichoderma.

Diseases: Sigatoka leaf spot, Panama wilt, Banana Bunchy Top Virus (BBTV).
Control: Disease-free suckers, timely spraying, removal of infected plants.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

Crop duration: 12–14 months after planting.

Harvest when fruits are light green to yellow and 75–80% mature.

Average yield: 25–30 tonnes/ha under good cultivation practices.

10. Post-harvest Handling

Harvest with 30–40 cm stalk for easy handling.

Ripen naturally in well-ventilated rooms or by using ethylene ripening chambers (avoid carbide).

Grading: Based on fruit size, color, and weight.

Packing: Use padded cartons or bamboo baskets to prevent bruising.

Storage: At 13–15°C and 85–90% RH, fruits remain fresh for 2–3 weeks.
