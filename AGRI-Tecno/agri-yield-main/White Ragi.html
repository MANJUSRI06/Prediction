🌱 White Ragi (Finger Millet – White Variety) Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

The field should be ploughed 2–3 times with country plough or tractor to get a fine tilth.

Incorporate well-decomposed farmyard manure (FYM) @ 8–10 tons/acre before the last ploughing.

Level the land properly to avoid waterlogging, since ragi is sensitive to standing water.

Prepare ridges and furrows or flat beds depending on irrigation availability.

2. Soil Type and pH

White ragi grows best in red loamy, sandy loam, or light black soils.

Soil should be well-drained; it does not tolerate waterlogging.

Ideal soil pH is 5.5–7.5, slightly acidic to neutral.

It can also grow in marginal and upland soils where other cereals fail, making it a hardy crop.

3. Climate Requirements

White ragi is a tropical crop, suited for both kharif (rainy season) and rabi (winter) cultivation.

Temperature range: 25–30°C is ideal.

Requires annual rainfall of 400–1000 mm, but it is drought-tolerant and survives dry spells.

Grows well up to altitudes of 1000–1800 m above sea level.

Sensitive to frost and prolonged waterlogging.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

Use pure white ragi seeds (local traditional varieties like Vellai Kezhvaragu in Tamil Nadu or Bili Ragi in Karnataka).

Seed rate: 10–12 kg per acre for broadcasting, 8–10 kg per acre for line sowing.

Seed treatment:

  • Treat seeds with Trichoderma viride @ 4 g/kg or Carbendazim 2 g/kg to protect from seed-borne diseases.
  • Biofertilizer coating with Azospirillum + Phosphobacteria improves germination and nutrient uptake.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

Time of sowing:

  • Kharif (June–July, with onset of monsoon)
  • Rabi (September–October under irrigated conditions)

Methods:

  • Broadcasting – Traditional method, but requires more seeds.
  • Line sowing – Preferred, as it gives higher yield and makes weeding easier.

Spacing: 30 cm between rows and 10 cm between plants. Thin seedlings at 2 weeks after germination.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

Apply FYM @ 8–10 tons/acre at land preparation.

Recommended NPK dose: 40:20:20 kg/acre.

Basal dose: 50% Nitrogen + full Phosphorus + full Potassium at sowing.

Top-dressing: Remaining 50% Nitrogen at 30–35 days after sowing (tillering stage).

Foliar spray of 2% urea at flowering stage can improve grain filling. Incorporation of biofertilizers enhances soil fertility.

7. Irrigation Management

White ragi is drought-tolerant but needs 2–3 critical irrigations for higher yield:

  • At germination stage (if rains are not adequate)
  • At tillering stage (~25–30 DAS)
  • At flowering and grain-filling stage (~45–60 DAS)

Avoid waterlogging; use furrow irrigation in heavy soils.

8. Weed and Pest Control

First weeding and thinning: 15–20 DAS. Second weeding: 30–35 DAS.

Weeds like Cynodon, Cyperus, and Echinochloa compete strongly. Use Pendimethalin 1.0 kg a.i./ha as pre-emergence herbicide or manual weeding.

Pests: Shoot fly and stem borer – control by spraying Neem oil or Dimethoate (if severe).

Diseases: Blast disease (leaf & neck blast) – spray Carbendazim 1 g/litre or Tricyclazole 0.6 g/litre at early symptoms.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

Crop duration: 100–120 days depending on season and variety.

Harvest when ears turn brown, grains are hard, and straw turns golden yellow.

Cut ear heads separately or harvest whole plants, then dry before threshing.

Expected yield: Grain yield: 8–12 quintals per acre (800–1200 kg). Straw yield: 15–20 quintals per acre (used as fodder).

10. Post-Harvest Handling

After harvest, dry ear heads for 3–4 days under sunlight.

Threshing can be done by beating with sticks or using a mechanical thresher.

Grains should be sun-dried to 8–10% moisture for safe storage.

Store in airtight bins or gunny bags in cool and dry godowns.

White ragi has good storability and is in demand for baby foods, malt, flour, and health products.
