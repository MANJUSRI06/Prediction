🌱 Velipala Jackfruit Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

Select well-drained land with good sunlight exposure.

Dig pits of 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft and fill with a mixture of topsoil + well-decomposed FYM (10–15 kg) + Neem cake (1–2 kg) before planting.

2. Soil Type and pH

Grows well in loamy and sandy loam soils.

Soil pH: 6.0 – 7.5 (slightly acidic to neutral).

3. Climate Requirements

Prefers warm and humid tropical climate.

Ideal temperature: 20°C – 35°C.

Requires annual rainfall of 1200–2500 mm but sensitive to waterlogging.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

Propagated by seeds, grafting, or budding.

For uniform fruit quality, softwooad grafting is recommended.

Treat seeds/planting material with Trichoderma or fungicide before sowing to prevent soil-borne diseases.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

Plant grafts or seedlings in prepared pits at 8 m × 8 m spacing (about 150 trees/ha).

Ensure staking for young plants to avoid wind damage.

6. Fertilizer Schedule

Apply FYM (20–25 kg/tree/year) at the beginning of monsoon.

NPK dosage (per year, per tree):

  • Nitrogen – 200 g
  • Phosphorus – 150 g
  • Potassium – 200 g

Apply in split doses during June–July and December–January.

7. Irrigation Management

Requires irrigation once in 10–15 days during summer.

Avoid waterlogging around roots.

Mulching with dry leaves/coconut husk conserves soil moisture.

8. Weed and Pest Control

Regular weeding in the first 2–3 years.

Common pests: fruit borers, mealy bugs – controlled by neem oil spray (3%).

Diseases: stem rot can be prevented with Bordeaux mixture (1%).

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

Trees start bearing fruits from 4th–5th year.

Peak harvesting season: March to June.

A mature tree yields 40–60 fruits annually (each 15–25 kg).

Yield: 20–25 tonnes/ha under good management.

10. Post-harvest Handling

Harvest when fruit emits characteristic aroma and produces a dull hollow sound when tapped.

Handle carefully to avoid skin injury.

Store fruits in cool, dry conditions (lasts 5–7 days at room temperature, 2–3 weeks in cold storage).

Suitable for processing into chips, jams, pulp, dehydrated flakes, and seeds for snacks.
