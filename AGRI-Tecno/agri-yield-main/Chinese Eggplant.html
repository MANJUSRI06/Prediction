🌱 Chinese Eggplant Cultivation Guide

1. Land and Soil Preparation

▼

Select well-drained fertile land with good organic matter.

Plough the field 2–3 times to get fine tilth and remove weeds/stubbles.

Raised beds or ridges are better, as Chinese eggplants are sensitive to waterlogging.

Apply 15–20 tons of well-decomposed farmyard manure (FYM) or compost per hectare during land prep.

2. Soil Type and pH

▼

Best grown in sandy loam or loam soils that are loose and rich in nutrients.

Soil pH: 5.5 – 6.5 (slightly acidic to neutral).

Avoid heavy clay soils as roots may not spread well and fruits become small.

3. Climate Requirements

▼

Prefers warm, humid climates with plenty of sunlight.

Ideal temperature range: 22–30°C for growth and fruiting.

Sensitive to cold and frost (below 15°C can reduce flowering).

Needs 6–8 hours of direct sunlight daily for maximum yield.

4. Seed Variety and Treatment

▼

Popular Chinese eggplant varieties:

  • Pingtung Long (Taiwan)
  • Orient Express F1
  • Purple Shine
  • Thai Long Green (variant)

Seed rate: 250–300 g per acre (nursery requirement).

Treat seeds with Trichoderma viride (4 g/kg) or carbendazim (2 g/kg) to prevent fungal issues.

Hot water treatment (50°C for 25 minutes) improves germination and disease resistance.

5. Sowing Method and Spacing

▼

Seeds are sown in raised nursery beds and transplanted after 25–30 days when seedlings are 12–15 cm tall.

Prepare beds enriched with FYM and sand for good drainage.

Spacing:

  • Row-to-row: 60 cm
  • Plant-to-plant: 45 cm

Plant population: 10,000–12,000 plants per acre (slightly higher than American type, as fruits are slender and lighter).

6. Fertilizer Schedule

▼

Apply 15–20 tons of FYM/compost before transplanting.

Fertilizer requirement per hectare: NPK: 100:60:60 kg

Application:

  • Apply full P & K + ⅓ N as basal dose.
  • Remaining N applied in 2–3 splits (30, 60, 90 days after transplanting).

Foliar sprays of Boron (0.2%), Zinc sulphate (0.5%), and Magnesium sulphate (0.5%) improve fruit quality and reduce flower drop.

7. Irrigation Management

▼

First irrigation just after transplanting.

Regular irrigation every 7–10 days in winter and 4–5 days in summer.

Avoid overwatering as roots are shallow and prone to rot.

Drip irrigation with mulching saves water and improves yield.

8. Weed and Pest Control

▼

First hand weeding at 20–25 days after transplanting, second at 45 days.

Mulching reduces weed growth and conserves soil moisture.

Major pests:

  • Fruit and shoot borer – use neem oil sprays or pheromone traps.
  • Aphids & whiteflies – manage with yellow sticky traps, neem seed extract, or imidacloprid (if severe).
  • Spider mites – common in hot dry climates, controlled with acaricides or miticides.

Diseases:

  • Bacterial wilt – rotate crops, avoid waterlogging.
  • Powdery mildew & leaf spots – use sulphur sprays or mancozeb.

9. Harvesting Time and Yield

▼

First harvest: 55–65 days after transplanting (slightly earlier than American type).

Harvest when fruits are slender, glossy, and tender (before they harden).

Fruits are usually 20–30 cm long, thin-skinned, and mild in flavor.

Harvest at 4–6 day intervals for continuous fruiting.

Yield potential:

  • 15–20 tons per hectare under normal management.
  • Hybrids may yield 25–30 tons per hectare.

10. Post-Harvest Handling

▼

Handle carefully as the thin skin bruises easily.

Grade fruits by length, shape, and color for better market value.

Can be stored at 10–12°C with 90–95% RH for 7–10 days.

Packed in ventilated crates or bamboo baskets for local markets.
